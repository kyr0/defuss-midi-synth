TinySoundFont is by Bernhard Schelling, based on SFZero by Steve Folta. Its MIT notice stays at the head of `native/tsf.h`, and the build writes it to `dist/assets/midi-synth.LICENSE.txt` beside the module. TimGM6mb is by Tim Brechbill (2004) and David Bolton (2010). Debian's copyright file and the GPL-2.0 text are kept in `vendor/soundfonts/`, and the build joins them into `dist/assets/TimGM6mb.LICENSE.txt` beside the SoundFont. Debian's archive at https://deb.debian.org/debian/pool/main/t/timgm6mb-soundfont/ keeps the source tarball (SHA-256 `af8f3a00e416dfb262bcaa904a1c84df04a51b72bbc1313aed012bc754bdf99b`). The bundled SoundFont is 5,969,788 bytes with SHA-256 `c5378b62028c920cb11e4803327983fee2f2cdff5dc89c708e39da417e51c854`.

`native/synth.c`, `native/fm.cpp`, the files of `native/profiles/` and the TypeScript sources are this project's code. They are under the MIT License ([LICENSE](LICENSE)), except `native/profiles/cqm.cpp` (GPL-3.0-or-later) and `native/profiles/cqm_adlmidi.cpp` (LGPL-2.1-or-later), as their SPDX lines state; `gf1_mixer.h` adapts GUSEMU32's MIT code and keeps its notice. The block renderer `nukedopl3_block.inc.c` modifies Nuked OPL3 and keeps its LGPL-2.1-or-later licence, as its header states. `fm-synth.wasm` and `sb16-cqm.wasm` link this code with libADLMIDI, so the package as a whole is distributed under GPL-3.0-or-later, as `package.json` declares; the LGPL-2.1-or-later and BSD-3-Clause parts permit that combination. The SoundFont is a data file the module loads at run time; it keeps its own GPL-2.0 licence inside the combined distribution. The synthetic sequence, the MIDI, RMI and MUS fixtures, the synthetic ECW waveset and GF1 patches, and the SoundFont the tests write are authored for this project; the package distributes no game soundtrack or commercial sample set.

libADLMIDI is by Joel Yliluoma (2010-2014) and Vitaly Novichkov (2015-2026); Nuked OPL3 by Nuke.YKT (2013-2020), its fast variant by Tony Gies (2026). The sources keep their headers; the build writes the MIT notices and the LGPL-2.1 and GPL-3.0 texts to `dist/assets/fm-synth.LICENSE.txt` beside the module. The embedded banks are libADLMIDI's (`docs/music/banks.ini` lists them with their sources). `tests/test.wopl` is libADLMIDI's own test bank.

Each profile module ships with its notice, `dist/assets/profiles/<profile>.LICENSE.txt`, which names the pinned source. For the LGPL-2.1 and GPL-3.0 parts of the profile modules, `sb16-cqm.wasm` and `roland-mt32.wasm`, the package carries the corresponding source and the means to relink: `native/` (the adapters and libADLMIDI), the Nuked CQM files and libmt32emu's source tree in the two archives of `vendor/sources/`, and `scripts/build-native-profiles.py`, which rebuilds both modules from the pinned commits. The SoundCarcass and GUSEMU32 notices travel in the same files; their licences ask for the notice, not for source.

The cores contain no ROM, waveset or patch data, and this project distributes none: not the Roland ROMs, the EMU8000 ROM or Creative's banks, not the ECW wavesets (Copyright 1997 and 1998 ENSONIQ Corp., from EMAC's OEM driver folders, which state no redistribution right), and not the Gravis patches (Copyright 1992 and 1993 EYE&I Productions and Advanced Gravis, no redistribution grant found). You supply them as packs, which `scripts/fetch-roms.py` and `scripts/profile-assets.py` keep in `profile-assets/`; git ignores that folder and every `*.dspack`. The ROM tables in `scripts/roms/` hold only file names, sizes and published hashes, each with its source: Munt's `ROMInfo.cpp`, MAME's drivers (commit `50edc8df0eadb1f2cd51505a5005578d76ee00db`), and TASVideos and AWE32Emu's notes for the EMU8000 ROM. The GF1 core follows the Gravis UltraSound SDK 2.10 documentation, and dosbox-staging (GPL-2.0-or-later) and TiMidity served as cross-checks of facts; no code of theirs is included.

The demo site's `docs/music/` holds game soundtracks in General MIDI from https://www.karlsitretro.com/game_midi, with their covers, the Q-Pop: Evolution im Weltraum files the author added (their text events credit K.-L. von Wendt and Lars Hammer). Their rights stay with their authors and publishers; they are not part of the npm package (`package.json` `files`), and publishing the site publishes them.
