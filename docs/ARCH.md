# Browser demo

The page is a DOS screen over Matrix rain (`style.css`, `player.css`). Its layout follows defuss-shadcn's record shop showcase: a site header, the release as the hero (the album's cover beside its title and tracklist), the crates of album sleeves pinned askew with their years on stickers, the instrument as a framed window, and a footer of columns. Text is set in VT323 and headings in Press Start 2P, both served from `fonts/` (OFL) rather than from Google. The gallery loads its catalog, covers, cores and banks from the same origin. Corners are square, frames phosphor green, shadows hard and unblurred. Two of defuss-shadcn's shaders draw: `matrix` behind the page, whose speed follows the music's level and bass, and `crt`, which shows the album's cover in its own colors on a slightly curved, scanlined tube. The sleeves are pixelated in blocks of 2 CSS pixels by an SVG filter (`#px`) on their `<img>`: a shader instance draws into a canvas of its own, which the album sleeves do not need for a still picture. Game playback shows the cover for 2.5 seconds before Bergium fades in to full opacity. Computer music and local files show the visualization as playback starts. Pause, Stop and the end of the last track fade it out. Tracks advance automatically within an album. Next advances past the final track to the next album. The visualizer setting applies across all three sources.

VERIFIED: `app.js` uses the public built package under `synth/`, so the interactive demo exercises the delivered facade and AudioWorklet (`scripts/e2e.mjs`). The static page needs no backend. Uploaded music, SoundFonts, WOPL banks and profile packs stay in browser memory.

The bank and preset selectors list the active engine's presets. A drum kit of bank 128 plays on channel 10, every other preset on channel 1, through `selectPreset`; the MT-32 profile plays them on channel 2, since its parts listen on channels 2 to 9 and channel 1 is silent. The piano maps pointer and keyboard input to MIDI events on that channel. Controls use acknowledged commands, so failures appear in the status line. The page records what the keys and Web MIDI keyboards play as MIDI messages with their times, the piano's preset first; a pause of more than two seconds with no note or pedal held shrinks to two seconds, so a recording left open does not grow into minutes of silence. Its download, and each track's WAV button, render on a separate offline synthesizer (`render.js`) with the engine and bank shown, a second of audio per step with a yield between steps, without disturbing the live one. Web MIDI input goes to `sendMidi` as it arrives; system messages are left out. The scope draws only while keys sound, from any source, and for 0.8 s after the last. The demo score is original material supplied with this project.

The music player (`player.js`, `player.css`) plays the albums of `music/index.json` (`scripts/index-music.mjs` writes it). Its controls are defuss-shadcn's Audio, Playlist Item, Toggle Group, Select and Slider markup and styles with the page's colors as tokens; `player.js` drives them itself, because the sound comes from the AudioWorklet and the Audio component's script drives an `<audio>` element. An analyser on the synthesizer's output sets the rain's speed: 0.6 in silence, up to 3 with loud music and a heavy bass. The shader's clock adds up its speed frame by frame, so a changed speed never makes the rain jump. On either FM engine, the OPL3 or the CQM, each album plays with its game's bank from the index, and a list of all banks shows it; a bank the user picks holds until another album plays, and a WOPL file of the user's stays until the user picks a bank. An album's list holds the tracks of all its editions, so the page needs no edition selector. The bar at the bottom (`progress.js`, defuss-shadcn's Progress field) shows the library's `onProgress` events for files above 1 MB and the WAV renders. Selecting a track, the play button and the sound card covers start audio themselves, so the page needs no separate power-on. The page labels its engines by hardware profile and opens on Warcraft II, on the SoundBlaster 128 with its 2 MB ECW waveset and with Bergium. Albums whose files state a wrong clock carry a `tempo` in the index, which the player passes to `loadMidi`. The selected sound card and variant stay active when choosing an album or advancing past the last track.

VERIFIED: Each variant’s manual trim feeds the FX section. The default compressor uses a 2:1 ratio at −18 dB and enables Auto level. After EQ, compression and reverb, the leveler tracks stereo K-weighted energy, targets −16 LUFS before the listening volume, caps corrections at ±12 dB and holds gain through quiet releases. A stereo-linked peak guard limits sample peaks. The final meter follows the listening volume. The EQ handles and knobs change the same filter parameters. WAV exports snapshot the controls and use the same graph in an offline audio context. A flat EQ, bypassed compressor and zero reverb mix give dry playback.

VERIFIED: Opening a local song shows a one-file playlist with its filename, duration and embedded sequence title. The player displays the available MIDI metadata and starts the local view with a black cover. Catalog and local rows share the transport and WAV exporter. Selecting a catalog cover brings its playlist and artwork back.

VERIFIED: A metering branch after the output gain measures RMS and K-weighted momentary LUFS over 400 ms and updates every 100 ms. The player also shows the sample rate, estimated delay and active voice count. The keyboard shows the channel format and the queued, active voice and late event counts.

VERIFIED: The Sound Card gallery shows five covers in one row above 720 pixels and wraps to three columns on smaller screens. Each cover selects its card and the last variant used for that card during this visit. The SB16 has OPL3 and CQM buttons. The PCI 128 defaults to 2 MB ECW and also offers 4 MB ECW and 8 MB ECW. The three other covers each select one pack. Release years come from the matching entries in `packs.json` and the SB16 entry in `devices.json`.

VERIFIED: Playback and WAV export use the selected variant’s `baseTrimDb` from `card-loudness.json`. That gain stays fixed across catalog tracks and MIDI files opened locally. A user-supplied SoundFont uses unity trim.

VERIFIED: The static server serves the contents of `docs/`. The gallery reads the selected file from `soundcards/` on demand and wraps raw ECW bytes in a DSPK pack for the PCI 128 core. AWE core URLs resolve beside their packs. The bundled cores reside in `synth/assets/`. Browser playback and WAV rendering use the same WASM reader and bank. The cache in `demo/card-cache.js` uses defuss-storage's directory adapter on OPFS. It checks the SHA-256 for each file before use, so a corrupt copy or a changed build triggers a fresh download. The gallery reports storage failures and can play the downloaded bytes in memory.

The progress bar is defuss-shadcn's Slider, whose script redraws the filled part only on the user's input: the player sets the fill itself, and holds the bar only while the user drags it, not while it has the focus.

The visualizer is Bergium (https://github.com/kyr0/bergium), vendored in `vendor/bergium/` because it is not published to npm. `demo/visualizer.ts` uses its low-level `createVisualizer` on one canvas; the page offers its Butterchurn pipeline ("Bergium") or none ("No Visuals"), Bergium by default. It hears the synthesizer's `source`, the signal before the volume slider, through a gain of 4, so the picture moves at any volume and four times as strongly as the output's level gave it. It rotates every 30 seconds through the 14 presets of `demo/bergium-presets.json`, which the build takes from butterchurn-presets' base pack into `visualizer/presets.json` (102 KB instead of the 646 KB pack), and shows each new track's title over the picture. The build bundles it into `visualizer/` (197 KB), which the page loads once audio runs.

The build copies defuss-shadcn's runtime and the stylesheets the page uses to `shadcn/`, with its licence. The page keeps its other controls in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript; a framework would add a second build lifecycle for a single screen without reducing the audio implementation. `build.mjs` copies the package files to `synth/`; edits belong in `src/`, not there. The copy is committed, because GitHub Pages serves this folder from `main` without a build step, so a change to `src/` needs `make build` before its commit.

VERIFIED: Gaming Crates and Computer Music Creates use defuss-shadcn virtual grids backed by defuss-dataview. Each search matches artist, album and track metadata in its own collection. The computer music index groups VGZ files by their artist folders. On selection, a worker decompresses the recording and renders its OPL register stream with Nuked OPL3. Its stereo output feeds the player effects and master gain.
